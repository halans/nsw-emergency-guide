For an immediate or life-threatening emergency, call **000**. Ask for Police, Fire or Ambulance, give your exact location and follow the call-taker’s instructions. Do not delay an emergency call to browse this directory.

<a className="phone-link" href="tel:132500">132 500</a>

## Police assistance and health advice

These are not substitutes for emergency response. If someone is seriously ill or injured, or a crime is happening now, call **000**.

### Police Assistance Line

<a className="phone-link" href="tel:131444">131 444</a>

Non-urgent police assistance, minor-crime reporting and general enquiries.

<div className="resource-caution">

**Important:** For emergencies or a crime in progress, call 000 instead.

### Poisons Information Centre

<a className="phone-link" href="tel:131126">13 11 26</a>

Expert phone advice for suspected exposure to poisons, including medicines, chemicals, cleaning products, bites and stings.

**Hours:** 24/7

<div className="resource-caution">

**Important:** Call promptly; do not wait for symptoms. If someone has stopped breathing or collapsed, call 000 for an ambulance.

</div>

## If a voice call is not accessible

[Accessible emergency calls and relay options](/accessible-help) explains 106 and links to the National Relay Service. **106 is for TTY/TDD emergency calls, not ordinary SMS.**

## Crisis support

For emotional distress, mental-health support or family/sexual violence support, see [crisis and personal safety](/crisis-support). If someone’s life is in immediate danger, call **000**.
